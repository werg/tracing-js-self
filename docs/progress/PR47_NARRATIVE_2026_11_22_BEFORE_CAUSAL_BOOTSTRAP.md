### 1. Recover a known native commit without repeating the operation

Producer `e7f8fd6` and native `350c1e6` implement explicit reconciliation after a commit reply is lost. Before dispatch, the producer saves the exact original JSONL request and the complete immediate precommit state. The native process independently retains the request, complete before/after state and exact reply. Recovery requires the same session, stream, execution generation, model, tokenizer, operation and request position. The native process reconstructs the entire adapter and compares every retained session, sibling, attempt, random state, setting and closed-session entry before returning the original reply.

The implementation also closes three less obvious windows:

- **Later mutations supersede earlier recovery points before running.** A successful commit cannot be restored after a later selection, child bootstrap, cancellation or other application mutation, even when that later operation did not produce another commit. Read-only checkpoint/status operations preserve the point; process EOF disposal is distinguished from an explicit application cancellation.
- **One execution lease belongs to one native child.** The child inherits and retains the exact process lock. Private object branding rejects fabricated leases, and the producer refuses to hand one lease to two children or reuse it after a child exits. A new child requires releasing and reacquiring ownership.
- **Serialization covers the storage wait.** The request remains exclusive from the first journal read/write through native acknowledgement. Caller mutation and concurrent initialization, checkpoint, cancellation or commit cannot alter the in-flight request. Fresh initialization and ordinary restore cannot bypass occupied journal history or change its generation.

Missing native intent, uncertain acknowledgement, changed bytes, model mismatch and superseded state refuse recovery. Selection replies are not replay authority. This increment recovers the native commit only; it does **not yet reconcile the composite host/history checkpoint or authorize unknown external work**.

## Lessons and acceptance rules

- A terminal history acknowledgement can yield to other work before close dispatch. Bind and atomically recheck both its next request boundary and complete native precheckpoint; a known closed result must never become a resumable driver or authorize another host action.
- An authenticated history prefix is not proof of the latest durable head. A newer unacknowledged pending record must block stale restoration.
- A durable native acknowledgement and a durable host/history checkpoint are different authorities. Saving one does not reconcile the other. A producer intent can also exist without its publication acknowledgement: from that attempted publication onward, a storage failure must fence later native actions even if the intended native operation never ran.
- Child reservation and initialization are separate mutations: a lost initialization reply cannot be repaired by retrying an older reservation. Each needs its own exact durable outcome and a complete acknowledged application cut.
- Counting target records is not proof of the labels and masks actually scored. Independently reconstruct positions from immutable prepared records, and reject jointly shifted planner/execution receipts. Stage changes archive prior objective-bound exposure rather than crediting it anew.
- A later read preserves a recovery point; a later mutation can invalidate it even without a newer commit. Supersession must be durable before the mutation runs.
- Process-lock descriptor duplication shares an owner. It cannot authorize parallel native children.
- Original source execution decides whether a module has an execution Promise and whether an await rejection becomes module failure. Fixture assumptions must agree with real source behavior.
- Eligible sources, prepared target records, planned targets and optimizer-scored occurrences are different denominators. Each requires an independently reconciled count. Two copies of the same planner omission are not complete coverage.
- Cache only immutable admission evidence under complete identities. Preserve model-dependent work and exact output equality checks.
- Every result names its exact source and inputs. Retain failures and cancelled attempts; never count a skip, unsupported case, missing asset or resource limit as a pass.
- Keep the complete edition target visible. Intermediate release requirements do not shrink it. Keep overlapping populations and overlapping test manifests separate.
- Update producer, consumer, contracts and fixtures together when semantics change. Historical receipts remain historical after executable changes.
- Do not change production defaults until complete replacement parity is demonstrated. Learned selection remains experimental until its separate held-out quality/cost gates pass.
